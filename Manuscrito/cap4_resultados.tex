\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter presents the quantitative results of every experiment carried out in this thesis. The presentation follows the same top-down order used to validate the modelling pipeline: \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:gaussianity_results}} reports the per-dimension Gaussianity moments of the DINOv2 embeddings; \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:feature_space_diagnostics}} reports complementary feature-space diagnostics (PCA spectral decay, squared Mahalanobis distance histograms against the theoretical $\chi^2$ reference, and pairwise KL divergence between class-conditional Gaussians); \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:covariance_results}} reports continual-learning metrics ($A_T$, $F_T$) for the family of covariance estimators and mixture-of-Gaussian variants on the three 10-task benchmarks and on the 30-task Joint stream; \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:df_results}} reports the four tables that summarise the generative--discriminative re-ranking strategies; \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:backbone_results}} ablates the choice of feature extractor; \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:calibration_results}} reports the Expected Calibration Error after temperature scaling; and \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:incremental_update}} reports the per-sample accuracy curves obtained when a new class is incorporated incrementally; and \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:calibration_metrics_results}} reports five additional probabilistic metrics (Accuracy, ECE, Confidence on Errors, Total Confidence, Log-Gap and Entropy) that complement the Expected Calibration Error analysis. All interpretation, comparison and discussion of these numbers is deferred to Chapter~\ref{ch:analysis}.

For every benchmark we report the average accuracy after the last task, $A_{T}$, and the forgetting score $F_{T}$ as defined in Chapter~\ref{methodolohy}. Whenever possible we report mean and variance over three independent runs (different task-order seeds); deterministic methods exhibit zero variance because the underlying Gaussian estimators are closed-form. State-of-the-art prompt-based competitors are reproduced from the published values reported in their respective papers. Methods proposed in this thesis are highlighted in grey. The best result per column is shown in bold.

\section{Feature-Space and Gaussianity Diagnostics}
\label{sec:feature_space_diagnostics}

Figure~\ref{fig:pca_eigenvalues} reports the spectrum of the empirical covariance matrix of the $\ell_2$-normalised DINOv2 embeddings for the three benchmarks. The eigenvalues exhibit a steep decay in approximately the first 50 principal components followed by a much more gradual decline, indicating that a substantial portion of the variance is captured by the leading components, while the remaining ones encode progressively finer-grained information. The three datasets share remarkably similar spectra, despite their different image domains and class budgets.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{imagenes/results/pca_eigenvalues.png}
	\caption{PCA eigenvalue decomposition of the $\ell_2$-normalised DINOv2 features for the three benchmarks (\textbf{a}: linear scale; \textbf{b}: logarithmic scale). Approximately 36--40\,\% of the total variance is concentrated in the first 50 components on every dataset.}
	\label{fig:pca_eigenvalues}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:mahalanobis_full_cov} reports the empirical distribution of the squared Mahalanobis distance under the per-class Full-Covariance Gaussian model for the three benchmarks, together with the theoretical $\chi^2_{d}$ ($d=768$) density. On every dataset the empirical distribution deviates significantly from the theoretical curve and collapses to a sharp spike around $\overline{n_c}-1$, where $\overline{n_c}$ is the average number of training samples per class ($\overline{n_c}\!\approx\!500$ for CIFAR-100, $\overline{n_c}\!\approx\!150$ for ImageNet-R, $\overline{n_c}\!\approx\!112$ for Caltech-256). The location of the spike thus scales linearly with the per-class sample budget, which is the fingerprint of rank-deficient covariance estimation ($\mathrm{rank}(\hat\Sigma_c) \le n_c - 1 < d$): the eigenvalues outside the spanned subspace are pinned to the regularisation floor $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ and the resulting Mahalanobis distance becomes degenerate.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{imagenes/results/mahalanobis_full_cov.png}
	\caption{Squared Mahalanobis distance histograms for the Full-Covariance model on (a) CIFAR-100, (b) ImageNet-R and (c) Caltech-256, overlaid with the theoretical $\chi^2_{d=768}$ density. On every dataset the empirical mass collapses to a spike near $\overline{n_c}-1$ because the estimated covariance is rank-deficient.}
	\label{fig:mahalanobis_full_cov}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:mahalanobis_diag_1d} confirms that the discrepancy is not a property of the Gaussian assumption itself but of the multivariate covariance estimate. The top row shows the Diag-Cov histograms on each benchmark: the empirical mass is broad and centred near the $\chi^2_{d}$ mode ($d=768$), but it is more dispersed than the theoretical curve, and the dispersion grows with the dataset's heavy-tailedness (CIFAR-100 $<$ ImageNet-R $<$ Caltech-256, in line with Table~\ref{tab:gaussianity_stats}). The bottom row plots the squared distance for a \emph{single} coordinate (1-D) against the $\chi^2_1$ reference: on all three datasets the per-coordinate distribution closely follows the theoretical curve, supporting the marginal Gaussianity claim. The mismatch in the multivariate setting therefore arises from the diagonal estimator ignoring off-diagonal correlations, not from a violation of the per-component normality.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{imagenes/results/mahalanobis_diag_and_1d.png}
	\caption{Squared Mahalanobis distance histograms across the three benchmarks. \textbf{Top row (a$_1$, a$_2$, a$_3$):} Diag-Cov histograms compared to the theoretical $\chi^2_{d=768}$ on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R and Caltech-256, respectively. \textbf{Bottom row (b$_1$, b$_2$, b$_3$):} single-component (1-D) histograms compared to the theoretical $\chi^2_1$. The 1-D distributions closely match the theoretical reference on every dataset, supporting per-coordinate Gaussianity; the multivariate dispersion in the top row reflects the unmodelled inter-coordinate correlations.}
	\label{fig:mahalanobis_diag_1d}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:kl_divergence} reports the inter-class separability of the Diag-Cov Gaussian models in terms of the KL divergence. For every benchmark, the average and minimum pairwise KL divergences $\mathrm{KL}(\mathcal{N}_a\,\|\,\mathcal{N}_b)$ are reported as functions of the number of classes considered $k$, computed over all $k(k-1)$ ordered class pairs in the first $k$ classes. Caltech-256 stands out with the largest average separability (around $\sim\!1000$ nats with up to 250 classes), CIFAR-100 stabilises around $\sim\!370$ nats with 100 classes, and ImageNet-R lies between the two at $\sim\!357$ nats with 200 classes. The minimum KL divergence (right panel) decays sharply on every benchmark---from several hundred nats with a handful of classes to below $100$ nats once the full class budget is considered---revealing the existence of confusable class pairs whose Gaussians strongly overlap.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{imagenes/results/kl_divergence.png}
	\caption{Inter-class separability under the Diag-Cov Gaussian model. (\textbf{a}) Average pairwise KL divergence and (\textbf{b}) minimum pairwise KL divergence, both as functions of the number of classes considered. The average separability remains essentially flat once a few dozen classes have been added, whereas the minimum decays sharply, reflecting the appearance of strongly overlapping class pairs.}
	\label{fig:kl_divergence}
\end{figure}

\section{Incremental Gaussian Model Update}
\label{sec:incremental_update}

Figure~\ref{fig:incremental_class_inclusion} illustrates how the Diag-Cov and Full-Cov class-conditional models behave when samples of a new class arrive progressively. After learning an initial set of nine base classes, an additional class is introduced and its training samples are incorporated in steps of $10\,\%$ of the available data. Two representative target classes per benchmark are reported, chosen to span the dataset's class index range. On Split CIFAR-100 (\textbf{a}), the Diag-Cov classifier reaches a strong plateau already with $10\,\%$ of the samples (reflecting the small number of parameters required to estimate $d=768$ diagonal variances), while Full-Cov improves more gradually and surpasses Diag-Cov once 40--70\,\% of the samples are available. On Split ImageNet-R (\textbf{b}), the limited number of training samples per class (${\sim}40$ after the train/test split for some classes) makes the Full-Cov estimator saturate early at a lower-than-CIFAR ceiling; both estimators improve gradually but the gap between Diag-Cov and Full-Cov stays small. On Split Caltech-256 (\textbf{c}), Full-Cov already dominates from the very first $10\,\%$ slice---reflecting the higher visual variability that benefits more from inter-coordinate correlations---and Diag-Cov tracks the Full-Cov curve at a 1--2 point lower plateau.
