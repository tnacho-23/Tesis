\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter presents the quantitative results of the experimental campaign in the following order: Section~\ref{sec:covariance_results} reports continual-learning metrics for the covariance estimators and mixture-of-Gaussian variants, including final-state confusion matrices and the effect of the log-normalisation constant; Section~\ref{sec:Gaussianity characterization_combined} characterises the feature space, consolidating score component distributions, marginal and multivariate Gaussianity tests (Section~\ref{sec:gaussianity_results}), inter-class separability via pairwise Bhattacharyya distance (Section~\ref{sec:interclass_results}), and the PCA spectral decay of the DINOv2 embeddings together with the accuracy under PCA-reduced representations (Section~\ref{sec:spectral_analysis}); Section~\ref{sec:covariance_structure_results} reports the covariance matrix conditioning and cross-class Frobenius analysis; Section~\ref{sec:df_results} covers the generative--discriminative re-ranking strategies; Section~\ref{sec:incremental_update} presents the sample efficiency and incremental class-inclusion curves; Section~\ref{sec:backbone_results} ablates the choice of feature extractor; and Section~\ref{sec:calibration_results_section} reports the probabilistic confidence and calibration metrics. All interpretation is deferred to Chapter~\ref{ch:analysis}.

For every benchmark we report the average accuracy after the last task, $A_{T}$, and the forgetting score $F_{T}$ as defined in Chapter~\ref{methodology}. Whenever possible we report mean and variance over three independent runs (different task-order seeds). Since the proposed Gaussian classifiers maintain only sufficient statistics $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_c, \Sigma_c)$ and do not involve parameter optimisation during incremental updates, the resulting classifier is independent of the specific class-presentation order; this explains the $\pm 0.00$ standard deviation reported for our methods throughout. State-of-the-art prompt-based competitors are reproduced from the published values reported in their respective papers. Methods proposed in this thesis are highlighted in grey. The best result per column is shown in bold.

\subsection{Score Components Distributions}
The diagonal-covariance Gaussian classifier assigns each test sample $\mathbf{x}$ to the class that maximizes the log-likelihood score:
\begin{equation}
	\mathrm{score}(\mathbf{x}, c)
	= \underbrace{\ln A_c}_{\text{(a) volume}} \;-\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,
	\underbrace{d_{\mathrm{mah}}(\mathbf{x},c)}_{\text{(b) shape}},
	\label{eq:score_components}
\end{equation}
where the two components are
\begin{align}
	\ln A_c &= -\tfrac{D}{2}\ln(2\pi) - \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\ln\sigma^2_{c,d},
	\label{eq:lnA}\\
	d_{\mathrm{mah}}(\mathbf{x},c) &= \sum_{d=1}^{D}\frac{(x_d - \mu_{c,d})^2}{\sigma^2_{c,d}}.
	\label{eq:dmah}
\end{align}
$\ln A_c$ depends only on the class variances (volume term); $d_{\mathrm{mah}}$ depends on the distance from the sample to the class mean weighted by the inverse variances (shape term). Each figure shows three panels: (a) the $C$ values of $\ln A_c$, one per class; (b) the $N_{\mathrm{val}} \times C$ pairwise distances $d_{\mathrm{mah}}(\mathbf{x},c)$, with every validation sample scored against every class; and (c) the corresponding log-likelihood scores.

Figures~\ref{fig:hist_cifar_val} and~\ref{fig:hist_cifar_val_full} show these distributions on the held-out validation set for CIFAR-100 under the Diag-Cov and Full-Cov estimators respectively.

Two complementary per-feature normality assessments are reported in Table~\ref{tab:gaussianity_normaltest}.
The D'Agostino--Pearson normaltest at $\alpha=0.05$ provides the strictest formal criterion; it combines standardised skewness and kurtosis into a $\chi^2_2$ statistic and is therefore more sensitive than inspecting either moment alone.
The relaxed criterion---$|\mathrm{Skew}|<1$ \emph{and} $|\mathrm{Kurt}|<1$ simultaneously---is a lenient but transparent indicator of near-Gaussianity.
With training splits of $\bar{n}_c\approx400$, $120$ and $90$ samples per class for CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R and Caltech-256 respectively, even minor deviations from perfect normality are detectable, so the normaltest is conservative.
$70$--$81\,\%$ of all (class, dimension) pairs pass the strict test, and the median $p$-value lies well above $0.05$ on every benchmark.
The relaxed criterion yields $82$--$98\,\%$ of pairs near-Gaussian in both shape moments simultaneously.

\subsubsection{Univariate Henze--Zirkler Test per Dimension}
\label{sec:uv_hz_gaussianity_results}

Table~\ref{tab:gaussianity_hz_univariate} and Figure~\ref{fig:thz_univariate_histograms} report the univariate HZ test applied independently to every (class~$c$, dimension~$j$) pair (Section~\ref{sec:method:gaussianity:hz_univariate}).

The reference point for interpreting the observed $T_{\mathrm{HZ}}$ values is the theoretical expectation under exact normality, $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{HZ}}] = 1-(1+2b^2)^{-1/2}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:hz_expected} with $d=1$), where the bandwidth $b^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^{0.4} n_c^{-0.4}$ depends only on the per-class sample size $n_c$. Larger classes have smaller $b^2$ and thus smaller $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{HZ}}]$: for CIFAR-100 ($n_c\approx 400$) the expected value is approximately $0.04$, while for Caltech-256 ($n_c\approx 80$) it rises to approximately $0.07$. A distribution that is perfectly Gaussian should therefore produce a $T_{\mathrm{HZ}}$ close to these values; values substantially above them indicate departures from normality.

\subsection{Inter-Class Pairwise Bhattacharyya Distance}
\label{sec:interclass_results}

Under the diagonal-covariance Gaussian model each class $c$ is represented by
$\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_c, \Sigma_c)$ with $\Sigma_c = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2_{c,1},\ldots,\sigma^2_{c,D})$.
The Bhattacharyya distance between two such distributions is

\begin{equation}
	D_B(i,j) =
	\frac{1}{8}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_j)^{\top}
	\bar{\Sigma}^{-1}
	(\boldsymbol{\mu}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_j)
	+
	\frac{1}{2}\ln\!\left(
	\frac{\det\bar{\Sigma}}{\sqrt{\det\Sigma_i\,\det\Sigma_j}}
	\right),
	\label{eq:bhattacharyya}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\bar{\Sigma} = (\Sigma_i + \Sigma_j)/2$.
Because both $\Sigma_i$ and $\bar{\Sigma}$ are diagonal, all matrix operations
reduce to element-wise arithmetic: $\det\Sigma = \prod_d \sigma^2_d$,
$\bar{\Sigma}^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}(2/(\sigma^2_{i,d}+\sigma^2_{j,d}))$, and
log-determinants become sums of logs.
This avoids costly dense matrix inversions and keeps computation in $\mathcal{O}(C \cdot D)$
memory per class pair.
$D_B = 0$ indicates complete distributional overlap; larger values correspond to
greater separability and a lower Bhattacharyya coefficient $\rho = e^{-D_B}$.

Figure~\ref{fig:bhattacharyya} characterises the inter-class separability as the
class set grows from $k=2$ to the full benchmark size.
The mean pairwise $D_B$ at the full class-set size is:
CIFAR-100 ($89.43$, $[\mathrm{P}_{10}{=}56.85,\,\mathrm{P}_{90}{=}126.30]$),
ImageNet-R ($84.81$, $[\mathrm{P}_{10}{=}49.45,\,\mathrm{P}_{90}{=}122.85]$),
and Caltech-256 ($220.61$, $[\mathrm{P}_{10}{=}113.63,\,\mathrm{P}_{90}{=}350.79]$).
The minimum pairwise $D_B$ at the full class-set size reaches
$3.57$ on CIFAR-100, $11.54$ on ImageNet-R, and $20.96$ on Caltech-256.

Pairs with $D_B < 20$  account for only $0.20\,\%$
($20/9\,900$) of CIFAR-100 pairs, $0.12\,\%$ ($48/39\,800$) of ImageNet-R
pairs, and $0.00\,\%$ of Caltech-256 pairs.
Broadening the threshold to $D_B < 50$  captures
$4.77\,\%$, $10.40\,\%$, and $0.15\,\%$ of pairs respectively.

\subsection{Conditioning Analysis}
\label{sec:conditioning_results}

Table~\ref{tab:conditioning} summarises the conditioning metrics of the regularised full covariance matrices $\hat{\Sigma}_c^{\text{reg}} = \hat{\Sigma}_c + 10^{-6}I$ computed on the training split (Section~\ref{sec:method:conditioning}). The three reported metrics characterise different aspects of how well-conditioned the per-class covariance is. The \textbf{condition number} $\kappa = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$ measures the spread between the largest and smallest eigenvalue; because it can span many orders of magnitude it is reported on a $\log_{10}$ scale. A high $\log_{10}(\kappa)$ means the matrix is nearly singular, so small perturbations in the data can cause large changes in the estimated inverse. \textbf{Degenerate dimensions} counts how many of the $d=768$ eigenvalues fall below the threshold $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{thr}}=10^{-4}$; these directions carry negligible variance and are effectively noise. \textbf{Effective rank} is the entropy-based soft count of informative directions (Eq.~\ref{eq:effective_rank}): unlike the hard rank it penalises unequal eigenvalue distributions, so a matrix whose variance is concentrated in a few directions will have a low effective rank even if many eigenvalues are technically non-zero.

\section{Sample Efficiency and Incremental Class Inclusion}
\label{sec:incremental_update}

Figure~\ref{fig:incremental_class_inclusion} illustrates how the Diag-Cov and Full-Cov class-conditional models behave when samples of a new class arrive progressively. After learning an initial set of nine base classes, an additional class is introduced and its training samples are incorporated in steps of $10\,\%$ of the available data. Two representative target classes per benchmark are reported, chosen to span the dataset's class index range. On Split CIFAR-100 (\textbf{a}), the Diag-Cov classifier reaches a strong plateau already with $10\,\%$ of the samples, while Full-Cov improves more gradually and surpasses Diag-Cov once 40--70\,\% of the samples are available. On Split ImageNet-R (\textbf{b}), both estimators improve gradually and the gap between Diag-Cov and Full-Cov stays small. On Split Caltech-256 (\textbf{c}), Full-Cov already dominates from the very first $10\,\%$ slice, and Diag-Cov tracks the Full-Cov curve at a 1--2 point lower plateau.

All quantities are computed at the last after-task checkpoint and averaged across the per-task entries of that row. The original CIFAR-100 source files for Diag-Cov and Full-Cov in the data folder were byte-identical (a copy-paste error); we regenerated the Full-Cov metrics from the cached DINOv2 features so the two columns now reflect distinct estimators.
