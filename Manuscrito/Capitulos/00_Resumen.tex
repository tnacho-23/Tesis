 \begin{center}
 	MITIGATING CATASTROPHIC FORGETTING IN  OBJECT CLASSIFICATION VIA GAUSSIAN CLASS REPRESENTATIONS
 \end{center}
 
 \noindent Traditional deep learning architectures collapse under non-stationary data streams, where incorporating novel categories demands computationally exhaustive retraining or triggers catastrophic forgetting. This thesis presents a gradient-free Class-Incremental Learning (CIL) framework that reframes lifelong visual recognition as a problem of closed-form density estimation rather than dynamic parameter optimization. By pairing a frozen DINOv2 foundation encoder with localized per-class multivariate Gaussian models estimated recursively via their sufficient statistics, new categories are integrated instantaneously without backpropagation, samples storage, or parameters optimization. Because historical class parameters are explicitly frozen, the framework eliminates representation drift and achieves zero catastrophic forgetting by construction. Evaluated across Split CIFAR-100, Split ImageNet-R, Split Caltech-256, and a highly heterogeneous 30-task Joint benchmark, this deterministic approach achieves classification accuracies competitive with or outperforming optimization-heavy, prompt-based state-of-the-art architectures, while adding negligible downstream computational overhead ($<0.3$ GFLOPs). Furthermore, a comprehensive statistical characterization of the foundation embedding space---utilizing formal univariate and multivariate normality tests, global spectral analysis, and inter-class Bhattacharyya distance diagnostics---empirically validates the core modeling assumptions. This geometric analysis reveals that the choice of optimal covariance parameterization is strictly governed by the interplay between localized sample density and the ambient feature dimensionality. While robust diagonal estimators excel under severe data scarcity by mitigating rank deficiency, a hybrid parameterization that decouples the shape and volume terms of the log-likelihood function yields a superior accuracy--calibration balance as the class sample budget expands.
