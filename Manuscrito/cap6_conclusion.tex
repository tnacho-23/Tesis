\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis revisited class-incremental learning in the context of modern visual foundation models, arguing that once representations are frozen and sufficiently discriminative the main challenge of CIL shifts from protecting model parameters to modelling class distributions in a stationary feature space. We formalised this shift by formulating incremental learning as recursive estimation of class-conditional Gaussian densities in the embedding space of a frozen DINOv2 backbone. Each class is represented by its sufficient statistics---a mean vector and a covariance matrix---which can be updated without backpropagation, replay buffers, or prompt parameters.

Empirically, this conceptually simple formulation achieves performance on Split CIFAR-100, Split ImageNet-R and Split Caltech-256 within a narrow margin of the strongest recent prompt-based methods, while requiring substantially lower computational overhead and providing a principled probabilistic interpretation. The proposed Hybrid covariance estimator---which combines a full-rank precision matrix for the Mahalanobis distance with a diagonal log-determinant for the volume term---delivers the best overall trade-off between accuracy and calibration, directly addressing the rank-deficiency pathology that limits plain full-covariance models in the low-sample regime. The empirical Gaussianity analysis confirms that foundation-model embeddings are approximately Gaussian at the per-coordinate level across all evaluated datasets, validating the modelling assumption even when per-class sample budgets are well below the ambient dimensionality of 768. These results suggest that, in the foundation-model era, incremental learning may be more naturally addressed through density estimation in stable representation spaces rather than continual parameter adaptation.

Beyond the specific Gaussian model, the findings point toward a broader research direction: treating continual learning as principled distribution modelling in stable representation spaces.

\section{Future Work}
Several natural extensions of the framework deserve further investigation.

\textbf{Structured covariance estimation.} The Hybrid estimator introduced in this thesis is a first step toward decoupling the shape and volume terms of the Gaussian log-likelihood. More principled approaches---such as low-rank plus diagonal (PPCA-style) decompositions or learned structured precision matrices---could further improve accuracy in the low-sample regime while preserving the interpretability and closed-form update properties of the current framework.

\textbf{Uncertainty calibration.} The calibration analysis of Chapter~\ref{ch:analysis} identifies the volume term as the primary source of over-confidence in the full-covariance branch. Temperature scaling mitigates this partially, but a more principled calibration strategy---for example, post-hoc Bayesian temperature estimation or conformal prediction intervals---could make the probabilistic outputs of the framework more reliable for safety-critical applications.

\textbf{Open-World Discovery.} Since the framework provides a well-calibrated predictive entropy and log-gap score for every input, implementing an Out-of-Distribution (OOD) threshold allows the system to flag unknown classes with minimal architectural changes. This addresses the objective of moving beyond a closed-set classifier toward an adaptive, ``discovery-ready'' system.

\textbf{Domain-incremental and cross-modal settings.} The current evaluation is restricted to visual classification with a fixed embedding space. An empirical assessment on text, speech, or multimodal embeddings would provide a valuable external test of the Gaussian assumption, and extending the framework to domain-incremental settings---where the input distribution shifts across tasks without a change in the label set---would broaden its practical scope.

\textbf{Real-Time Object Detection.} While previous experiments utilising a Region Proposal Network (RPN) \cite{Ren2015} yielded high localisation accuracy, the computational overhead of a dual-model pipeline (RPN + feature extractor) compromises inference speed. By leveraging the spatial tokens already present in the DINOv2 backbone, regions of interest can be identified via simple mathematical decomposition, keeping the system lightweight and consistent with the unsupervised nature of the feature extractor.
