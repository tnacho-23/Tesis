	\textbf{\MakeUppercase{Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting in Object Classification via Gaussian Class Representations

\end{center}

This work proposes a modular Continual Learning framework for object classification that decouples feature extraction from statistical classification. A frozen DINOv2 backbone provides stable high-dimensional embeddings, and each class is independently modelled by a Gaussian distribution estimated in closed form. New categories are incorporated by updating only the sufficient statistics of the new class, leaving all previously learned prototypes unmodified and requiring no past-sample storage. Evaluated on four class-incremental benchmarks, the framework matches or surpasses the prompt-based state of the art, reaching $94.4\,\%$ accuracy on Caltech-256. A rigorous statistical characterisation of the embedding space validates the Gaussian modelling assumptions and links the per-class sample regime to the optimal choice of covariance estimator.

\begin{abstract}
Traditional deep learning models struggle under non-stationary data: incorporating new classes demands exhaustive retraining or incurs catastrophic forgetting. This thesis introduces a gradient-free Class-Incremental Learning framework that combines a frozen DINOv2 backbone with independent per-class Gaussian models estimated in closed form. New categories are added by computing the mean and covariance of their embeddings in the fixed feature space; previously learned class prototypes are never modified and no past samples need to be retained. The framework therefore achieves zero catastrophic forgetting by construction. Evaluated on Split CIFAR-100, Split ImageNet-R, Split Caltech-256, and a demanding 30-task Joint benchmark, it surpasses every prompt-based baseline on Caltech-256 ($94.4\,\%$ vs.\ $92.5\,\%$) and matches state-of-the-art accuracy on the remaining benchmarks. A comprehensive statistical characterisation of the DINOv2 embedding space---encompassing univariate and multivariate Gaussianity tests, spectral analysis, inter-class Bhattacharyya distances, and covariance conditioning analysis---validates the modelling assumptions and demonstrates that the optimal covariance estimator is governed by the ratio of per-class samples to feature dimensionality: diagonal models excel in the severely under-sampled regime, while the hybrid estimator, which decouples the shape and volume terms of the log-likelihood, provides the best accuracy--calibration balance as the sample budget grows.
\end{abstract}
